Personality Rights and Artificial Intelligence: A Doctrinal Analysis of Judicial Protection

How the Delhi High Court has protected a person's name, face and voice between September 2021 and August 2026, and how that protection changed once deepfakes, voice clones and AI chatbots arrived.

Court: High Court of Delhi Period: 01-09-2021 to 31-08-2026 Judgments coded: 20 Team Persona: Shahnoor Sheikh, Ankita, Tanishkaa

The short answer

Personality rights in India still have no statute behind them. Everything in this dataset was built by judges, almost all of it by single judges at the interim stage. Read in date order, the 20 decisions tell a story in four phases: the court first confirmed a commercial right over celebrity identity, then drew limits around free speech and death, then stretched the right to cover AI misuse, and finally, from late 2025, began trimming overbroad claims so that parody, criticism and fan culture survive.

    Litigation grows sharply. There is nothing from late 2021, one order in 2022, and then a steady climb to seven orders in just the first eight months of 2026. The selection understates the real jump: the court's website shows dozens more celebrity suits in 2025 and 2026 that we did not code.

    2. Outcomes by year

    Allowed, partly allowed or dismissed, stacked by year

    Plaintiffs almost always win something: 18 of 20 orders give at least some relief. The change is in how much. Both outright refusals came in 2023. After that, the share of partly allowed orders rises, from one in 2024 to three of seven in 2026, as the court carves out parody, fan pages and political satire.

    3. Orders that expressly deal with AI

    The injunction or reasoning covers AI, deepfakes, voice cloning or AI chatbots

    Anil Kapoor (September 2023) is the turning point. Before it, no order in the dataset mentions AI. From 2024 every order does, until the two 2026 calibration orders (Alakh Pandey and Janhvi Kapoor), which focus on overbreadth rather than technology.

    Doctrinal trends

    From products to synthetic media

    • 2022: the harm is a stranger selling goods or lotteries with a celebrity's image (Amitabh Bachchan, relying on Titan Industries).
    • 2023 to 2024: the harm becomes technological: morphing, AI voice imitation, an AI chatbot of Jackie Shroff, doctored health ads using a surgeon's face.
    • 2025 to 2026: AI is now a standard clause. Injunctions name "AI, generative AI, machine learning, deepfakes and face morphing", and target AI companion chatbots (Sonakshi Sinha) and paid voice-cloning tools (Allu Arjun).

    From celebrities to anyone with a public persona

    • Protection spreads beyond film stars to a heart surgeon, a news anchor, a spiritual leader, an ed-tech founder and two MPs.
    • The legal anchor also shifts, from common-law passing off and endorsement value to dignity and privacy under Article 21 (Anil Kapoor, Shashi Tharoor).

    New remedies for the internet

    • John Doe orders against unknown uploaders become routine.
    • "Dynamic+" injunctions (Sadhguru) let new mirror sites be blocked without fresh orders.
    • Platforms are given 36 to 72 hour takedown deadlines and told to disclose uploaders' details.

    The counter-trend: calibration

    • Parody and lampooning carved out (Karan Johar, relying on D.M. Entertainment).
    • Whole fan pages not taken down (Hrithik Roshan, Janhvi Kapoor).
    • Political satire protected; "politicians must not be thin-skinned" (Raghav Chadha).
    • Relief limited to three categories: vulgar portrayal, monetisation, impersonation (Alakh Pandey); blanket order on 6,884 URLs refused (Janhvi Kapoor).

    Precedent use mirrors these phases. Early orders lean on R. Rajagopal and Titan Industries. Mid-period AI orders lean on Anil Kapoor (cited as the main authority in three later orders). The calibration orders go back further, to D.M. Entertainment v. Baby Gift House (2010) and its free-speech exception. It is the main authority in four orders, starting with Jackie Shroff (2024), where the court left a fan's "Thug Life" video online, and then Karan Johar, Hrithik Roshan and Alakh Pandey. Several 2026 orders cite no personality-rights precedent at all, a sign the court now treats the right as settled at the interim stage.

    Method and limits

    We searched the Delhi High Court judgment portal and Indian Kanoon for "personality rights", "publicity rights", "deepfake", "voice cloning" and "artificial intelligence", and used legal news reports to find orders we had missed. We kept reasoned orders only and picked 20 that span the period, including every refusal we found. Each order was coded in the Dataset template; the full data is in data.csv and data.xlsx.

    Almost every decision here is an interim order by a single judge, often passed ex parte. None is a final trial judgment and none comes from a Division Bench, so the "law" described is provisional. Twenty orders are a sample, not a census, and the per-year counts reflect our selection as much as the court's docket.
